A glass of tap water that tastes faintly of chlorine, white crust building up around faucet aerators, or a water softener that can't keep up with scale on shower doors all point to the same root issue: water quality, not just water pressure or temperature. Corona homes draw from a blend of Colorado River and local groundwater sources that run higher in dissolved minerals than many parts of Southern California, and that mineral load is exactly what a reverse osmosis system is built to strip out at the point of use.

## What Reverse Osmosis actually involves

Reverse osmosis forces tap water through a semi-permeable membrane under pressure, rejecting dissolved solids, sodium, nitrates, and many other contaminants that carbon filtration alone won't touch. A typical residential setup includes a sediment pre-filter, a carbon pre-filter, the RO membrane itself, a storage tank, and a dedicated faucet or a connection into the home's main line for whole-house treatment. Under-sink systems for drinking water and cooking are the most common install in Corona homes; whole-house RO is a bigger project involving larger membranes, booster pumps in some cases, and integration with existing plumbing. Installation on a standard under-sink unit usually runs a few hours; whole-house systems take longer depending on access to the main line and existing plumbing layout.

## Our process

1. **Water quality test.** We check TDS (total dissolved solids), hardness, and chlorine/chloramine levels at the tap to confirm what the water actually needs treated and to size the system correctly.
2. **System design and sizing.** Based on household water use, fixture count (for whole-house systems), and the test results, we spec membrane capacity, pre-filter stages, and storage tank size rather than defaulting to a one-size-fits-all unit.
3. **Installation.** This includes the drain saddle connection, feed water valve, storage tank, and air-gap faucet (or whole-house tie-in), done to code so there's no cross-connection risk with the home's potable supply.
4. **Pressure and output testing.** Once installed, we verify flow rate at the faucet, check for leaks at every fitting, and run a post-install TDS reading to confirm the membrane is rejecting at the expected rate.
5. **Maintenance scheduling.** We walk through filter change intervals and membrane replacement timing so performance doesn't quietly degrade over the following year.

## What separates a good Reverse Osmosis install from a bad one

The most common mistake in RO installs is undersizing the system for household demand, which leaves the storage tank unable to keep up and homeowners waiting on slow-filling glasses. A close second is skipping or skimping on pre-filtration: without adequate sediment and carbon stages ahead of the membrane, chlorine and particulates shorten membrane life dramatically, sometimes cutting a 2-3 year membrane down to months. Drain line connections matter more than most homeowners realize, too. A drain saddle installed without an air gap or installed on the wrong side of a trap can create a backflow path into the potable water system, which is both a code violation and a real contamination risk. A careful installer also accounts for incoming water pressure. Corona's municipal pressure varies by neighborhood and elevation, and RO membranes have a minimum pressure threshold to work efficiently, so pressure that's too low means a booster pump needs to go into the design from the start rather than as an afterthought.

## What does Reverse Osmosis cost?

Costs vary by system type, household size, and whether the install is a simple under-sink unit or a whole-house system tied into the main line. Every property's plumbing layout and water quality numbers are different, which is why we provide a written scope and price before any work begins rather than quoting off a phone call.

Homeowners insurance typically does not cover reverse osmosis installation, since it's a water quality upgrade rather than damage repair. If an RO system needs replacing because a covered event, like a burst supply line, damaged the existing unit, that portion of the loss may be covered, and we'll document it for the claim.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Corona's water hardness and mineral content stay fairly consistent year-round since it's sourced from a blended municipal supply rather than seasonal runoff, so RO performance doesn't swing much with the seasons the way, say, furnace demand does with winter cold snaps. What does matter locally is incoming water pressure, which can vary between older neighborhoods with aging mains and newer developments on upgraded infrastructure, and that's a factor we check at the time of system design rather than assuming it's uniform across the city.
