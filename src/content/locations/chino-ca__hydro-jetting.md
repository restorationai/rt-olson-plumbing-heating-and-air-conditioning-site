## Why Chino Properties See Hydro Jetting Issues

Chino's housing stock is a mix: older ranch-style homes from the city's agricultural era, when it was known as a major dairy region, sitting alongside newer tract developments built from the 1980s onward. The older homes often still run original clay or cast iron lateral lines, which develop rough interior surfaces over decades. Grease, soap scum, and mineral scale from the Inland Empire's naturally hard water cling to those rough walls far more aggressively than they would in a smooth PVC line, and that buildup narrows the pipe gradually until a slow drain becomes a full stoppage. Newer construction isn't immune either: expansive clay soil shifts with the wet-dry seasonal swing, and that movement puts stress on pipe joints that eventually let roots in or cause bellies where waste collects. A snake can punch through either problem temporarily, but it doesn't address the buildup coating the rest of the line.

## Our Hydro Jet Drain Cleaning Process in Chino

We start with a camera inspection, because jetting the wrong line pressure into a compromised clay or Orangeburg pipe can do more harm than good, and older Chino properties sometimes still have segments of that outdated material. Once we confirm the line can handle it, we feed a high-pressure hose down the cleanout and use rotating nozzles calibrated to the pipe diameter and material: lower pressure and a fan pattern for older clay, higher pressure for modern PVC or cast iron that can take it. The water scours grease, scale, and root hair off the pipe wall and flushes it downstream rather than just drilling a path through the middle of the clog. For restaurant and food-service lines in Chino's commercial corridors, grease line jetting is often the only method that keeps a line open between scheduled cleanings, since snaking alone just reopens a channel through hardened grease that reseals within weeks.
