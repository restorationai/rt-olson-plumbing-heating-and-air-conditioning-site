**Dealing with a drain that backs up no matter how many times you plunge it?** In San Bernardino, that's often not a clog near the fixture at all, it's buildup or root intrusion further down the line, and a snake just punches a hole through the blockage instead of clearing it. Hydro jet drain cleaning scours the full diameter of the pipe with high-pressure water, which matters more here than in newer developments because so much of the city's plumbing was installed decades before anyone thought about tree root pressure or grease buildup standards.

## Why San Bernardino Properties See Drain Line Buildup and Root Intrusion

San Bernardino sits in a valley ringed by the San Bernardino Mountains, and that geography shapes the soil underneath most properties: it shifts seasonally with the valley's dry summers and the occasional heavy winter rain, and that movement puts stress on older sewer laterals. A lot of the city's housing stock dates back to the mid-20th century, when clay and cast iron pipe were standard. Those materials develop hairline cracks over time, and mature street trees, common throughout the city's older residential blocks, send roots straight toward that moisture. Add in the valley's summer heat, which dries out surface soil and pushes root systems deeper in search of water, and you get a recurring pattern: slow drains that clear with a snake for a few months, then return with worse buildup each time. Restaurants and commercial kitchens along the city's busier commercial corridors deal with a related but separate problem: grease line buildup that a standard cable can't touch, which is why grease line jetting is a regular call for us in this market.

## Our Hydro Jet Drain Cleaning Process in San Bernardino

We start with a camera inspection before any water goes down the line. In a city with this much aging clay and cast iron pipe, that step isn't optional, jetting a pipe that's already fractured or offset can make things worse instead of better. Once we've confirmed the line can handle it, we run a hydro jetting nozzle through the pipe at a pressure calibrated to the material and condition we find, cutting through grease, scale, and root intrusion rather than just pushing it along. For older residential laterals, we typically use a lower pressure setting to protect brittle joints; for commercial grease lines and larger-diameter municipal-connected lines, we run higher pressure passes to strip buildup back to bare pipe. We follow up with a second camera pass so you can see the line is actually clear, not just moving water again for a few weeks.
