## Why Fontana Drains Clog Faster Than You'd Expect

Fontana sits on the alluvial fan sloping down from the San Gabriel Mountains, which means a lot of properties have clay-heavy or compacted soil that shifts with the wet winter and bone-dry summer swings typical of the Inland Empire. That soil movement puts stress on buried sewer laterals, especially the older clay and cast iron lines common in homes built during Fontana's postwar growth years. Add mature trees along older residential streets, and you get roots that find their way into pipe joints looking for moisture during the dry months. Grease is the other repeat offender, whether it's a family kitchen pouring bacon fat down the disposal or a restaurant line on one of Fontana's commercial corridors running sluggish by mid-shift. Both problems share the same flaw: a cable can open a channel through the blockage, but grease film and root hairs regrow against the pipe wall within weeks. Jetting strips the pipe back to bare surface, which is why it's the standard recommendation for sewer lines that clog on a pattern rather than as a one-off.

## Our Hydro Jet Drain Cleaning Process in Fontana

We start every job with a camera inspection, because jetting a line with a structural crack or a bellied section can push water into the surrounding soil instead of clearing the pipe. Once we've confirmed the line can handle pressure, we feed a specialized nozzle downstream through a cleanout, letting reverse-facing jets pull the hose forward while blasting water against the pipe walls on the way back out. For older Fontana homes with narrow or unusually routed clay laterals, we calibrate pressure to the pipe material rather than running every line at maximum PSI, since clay and brittle cast iron need a gentler approach than modern PVC. For grease-heavy kitchen lines, we often run a second pass with a degreasing attachment to break down buildup the first pass loosened. A post-job camera run confirms the line is open end to end, not just clear at the access point, which is the detail that separates a real jetting job from a quick clear-and-go.
