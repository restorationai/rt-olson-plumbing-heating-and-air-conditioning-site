**Grease, scale, and root intrusion behave differently in Ontario's older sewer laterals than they do thirty miles away.** A snake clears a path through a clog, but it doesn't touch the slick film of grease or the fine sediment clinging to the pipe wall, which means the same backup shows up again in a few months. Hydro jet drain cleaning blasts that buildup off the pipe wall entirely, which matters in a city where a lot of the drain and sewer infrastructure predates current code and sees heavy seasonal stress from Inland Empire heat and intermittent storm runoff.

## Why Ontario Properties See Drain Line Buildup

Ontario's housing stock spans everything from early-20th-century homes near the historic downtown core to large-lot properties built during the citrus-ranch era to newer construction further south. Older clay and cast iron laterals common in the established parts of the city are more prone to root intrusion, especially where mature trees were planted close to the sewer line decades before anyone mapped it. The region's clay-heavy soil also shifts with the wet-dry cycle typical of Southern California, which can misalign older pipe joints just enough to invite roots or trap sediment. On top of that, a fair number of Ontario's commercial corridors run older grease lines behind restaurants and food service tenants, and those lines narrow steadily if they aren't jetted on a schedule.

## Our Hydro Jet Drain Cleaning Process in Ontario

We start with a camera inspection to confirm what we're dealing with before we put water pressure to it. A root-choked clay lateral gets handled differently than a grease-lined commercial kitchen drain, and jetting the wrong way on an already-compromised pipe can turn a clog into a bigger repair. Once we know the pipe material and condition, we run a jetting hose down the line and work the nozzle from the far end back toward the access point, so debris is driven out rather than packed further down. For properties with older or fragile clay piping, we adjust pressure to clear buildup without stressing joints that have already started to shift. We finish with a second camera pass to confirm the line is actually open, not just temporarily cleared.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of drain work in Ontario's older neighborhoods: mature trees planted along property lines decades ago have often grown root systems that follow the path of least resistance, which is usually the moisture inside an aging clay sewer lateral. A line that backs up every six to twelve months on a property with large, established trees nearby is a strong sign of recurring root intrusion rather than a one-time clog, and jetting combined with a camera inspection is usually the more durable fix compared to repeated snaking.
