## Why Yorba Linda Pipes Clog the Way They Do

Much of Yorba Linda was built out from the 1960s through the 1980s, and a lot of that original cast iron and clay sewer pipe is still in the ground. Clay tile joints were never sealed against root intrusion the way modern PVC is, and Yorba Linda's eucalyptus, pepper, and oak trees send roots looking for any moisture source they can find, including a 50-year-old sewer joint. Add in the clay-heavy soil common across inland Orange County, which expands and contracts with the seasons and can shift pipe sections just enough to open a gap, and you've got a city where root intrusion and grease buildup are the two most common reasons a drain snake keeps coming back out with the same problem.

## How Hydro Jetting Works on Yorba Linda's Older Lines

A cable auger drills a channel through a clog and calls it done. Hydro jetting uses a flexible hose and a high-pressure water stream to scour the full interior wall of the pipe, cutting root intrusion back to the pipe wall and stripping grease, scale, and sludge rather than just boring a path through the middle. Before we run water at pressure, we run a camera through the line to confirm the pipe's material and condition, since older clay or Orangeburg pipe needs a different pressure setting than newer PVC. On a Yorba Linda home with original 1970s clay laterals, that inspection step matters: jetting a compromised clay joint at full pressure without first checking the pipe's condition can turn a slow-draining line into a bigger repair.
