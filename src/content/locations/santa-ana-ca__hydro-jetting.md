## Why Santa Ana's Drain Lines Need More Than a Snake

Santa Ana sits on mostly flat ground, which means gravity does less of the work moving wastewater than it does in hillier parts of the region. Slower flow gives grease, soap scum, and mineral scale more time to cling to pipe walls, and over the decades that buildup narrows the inside diameter of a line until even small amounts of paper or food waste cause a backup. The city's older residential blocks also carry mature street trees, and tree roots are drawn to the condensation and nutrients inside a leaking or loose clay joint. A cable can punch a hole through a root mass and make a drain run again for a few weeks, but it leaves the rest of the blockage, and the scale, stuck to the pipe wall. That's the gap hydro jetting is built to close.

## Our Hydro Jet Drain Cleaning Process

We start with a camera inspection, which matters more in Santa Ana than in newer suburbs because we need to know whether we're dealing with clay, cast iron, or ABS before we dial in pressure. Older clay and cast iron take a different jetting approach than PVC: we calibrate the nozzle pressure and flow pattern so the stream scours grease and scale off the pipe wall without stressing a joint that's already decades past its original install. Once we know what we're working with, the jetting hose runs the full length of the line, cutting through root intrusion, grease, and mineral buildup and flushing it toward the main.
