## What are the signs of a main sewer line clog?

A main line clog shows itself through multiple fixtures acting up at the same time, not just one slow drain. The clearest signs are a toilet that gurgles or bubbles when the washing machine drains, water backing up into a shower or tub when another fixture runs, and a sewage smell or standing water near the outdoor cleanout cap.

Other red flags: the lowest drain in the house (often a laundry sink, shower, or floor drain) is the first to back up, since it sits closest to the blockage and water finds the lowest exit point available. If you lift the cap on your yard cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the side of the house or along the property line, and see water sitting at or near the top, the clog is downstream of that point, in the main line itself.

A single slow drain that only affects one sink or tub, with no gurgling elsewhere, is a localized clog. That distinction matters because it changes both the fix and who you call.

## How is a main line clog different from one clogged drain?

A single drain clog blocks one branch line serving one fixture; a main line clog blocks the shared pipe every fixture in the house drains into before it reaches the street. A handheld or small-diameter auger can usually clear a branch drain clog in a sink or shower trap. A main line clog needs a larger cable machine or hydro-jetter fed through the main cleanout, since the pipe is bigger (typically 4 inches) and the blockage can be anywhere between the house and the city connection, sometimes 40 to 80 feet away.

Trying to clear a main line clog with a store-bought drain snake rarely works and can push the problem further down the line without actually opening it. ## Is a sewer backup in Corona the city's problem or mine?

In Corona, as in most California cities, the homeowner owns and maintains the private sewer lateral running from the house to the public main under the street; the city owns and maintains only the main line itself. That lateral, including the section under your yard, driveway, and parkway strip, is your responsibility to clear and repair, which is why a main line clog usually means calling a plumber rather than the city.

There is one exception worth checking before you call anyone: if sewage is surfacing from a street manhole, or multiple homes on your block are backing up at the same time, the blockage may be in the public main itself. In that case, Corona's Public Works department handles it, not a private plumbing contractor. If it is only your house, the lateral and the fix are on you.

## Are tree roots actually causing this?

Tree roots are a leading cause of main line clogs in Corona's older neighborhoods, especially streets lined with mature trees near the historic Circle District around Grand Boulevard, where clay sewer laterals were common before PVC became standard. Roots find their way in through small cracks or joint gaps in aging pipe, then grow into a dense mat that catches paper and grease until the line is effectively blocked.

Newer developments around areas like Eagle Glen and Dos Lagos, built with PVC laterals, see far fewer root intrusions, though a shifting or settling pipe joint can still let roots in over time. A sewer camera inspection, run through the cleanout after the clog is cleared, confirms whether roots, a bellied (sagging) section of pipe, grease buildup, or a cracked joint is the actual cause, so the repair addresses the real problem instead of just buying a few months before it backs up again. [Sewer camera inspection](/services/sewer-camera-inspection/) is the step that turns a one-time clearing into a long-term fix.

## What should you do before the plumber arrives?

Stop using water in the house immediately, including toilets, washing machines, dishwashers, and showers, since any added water has nowhere to go but back up. Locate your main cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation on the side of the house or out by the property line, so the technician can get to work the moment they arrive instead of searching for it.

Do not pour a chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up fixture. On a main line clog it rarely reaches the actual blockage, and it leaves caustic liquid sitting in standing water that a technician then has to handle. Backed-up sewer water is considered Category 3, or grossly contaminated, water, so keep kids and pets away from any area where it has surfaced and avoid direct contact without gloves.

If water has backed up onto flooring beyond what a mop can handle, document it with photos before cleanup starts; most standard homeowners policies do not automatically cover sewer backup damage unless a water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement was added to the policy, so check your coverage or contact your insurer directly rather than assuming it is covered.
