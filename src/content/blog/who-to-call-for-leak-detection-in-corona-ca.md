## What does a professional leak-detection visit actually involve?

A real leak-detection visit isolates the plumbing system zone by zone, then uses listening and imaging equipment to pinpoint the leak before any cutting starts. The technician isn't guessing where to dig, they're narrowing the search methodically.

A typical visit looks like this: the technician shuts off fixtures one group at a time and watches the water meter to confirm whether the leak is on the supply side or the drain side. From there, acoustic listening rods pick up the distinct hiss of pressurized water escaping underground or inside a wall cavity, since a pressurized leak sounds different from a gravity drain leak. A thermal camera can flag a cooler patch on a slab floor where water is wicking through concrete. For suspected toilet leaks, a dye tablet dropped in the tank settles the question in ten minutes, if color shows up in the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

## What are the warning signs that point to a hidden leak?

A sudden jump in your water bill with no change in household water use is the single most reliable sign of a hidden leak. Compare two or three recent bills side by side; a steady upward creep with no explanation (no new pool, no extra houseguests) points to a leak somewhere in the system.

Other signs worth acting on:

- A hissing or trickling sound inside a wall or under the floor when every fixture is off
- A warm spot on an otherwise cool slab floor (often a sign of a leaking hot-water line)
- Water pooling at the base of a wall with no visible source, or a stretch of unusually green grass in an otherwise dry yard
- A water meter dial that keeps spinning after you've shut off every fixture in the house

Any one of these on its own is worth a diagnostic visit. If you're also noticing cracked tile, a sloped floor, or a mildew smell near the slab, read through [the warning signs of a slab leak](/blog/signs-of-a-slab-leak/), since those point to a more advanced version of the same problem.
