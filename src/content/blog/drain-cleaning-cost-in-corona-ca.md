**TL;DR:** Drain cleaning in Corona typically costs $100 to $300 to clear a single sink, tub, or shower drain with a cable machine, $150 to $350 to snake a main sewer line through an exterior cleanout, and $350 to $900 for hydro jetting or root cutting on a main line with grease buildup or root intrusion. The exact number depends on how hard the clog is to reach, whether your home has an accessible cleanout, and whether the call happens during the day or after hours. Get the main-line price in writing before anyone starts working, not after.

If your bathroom sink has been draining slower every week, or your South Corona home's main line backed up into the shower floor last night, you need a number before you let anyone near it. A single bathroom drain clears for a very different price than a main sewer line choked with roots near the old clay lateral under your front lawn. The method, the access point, and what's actually causing the clog all move the final bill.

## How Much Does Drain Cleaning Cost in Corona, CA?

Most single-fixture clogs (a slow kitchen sink, a backed-up tub, a gurgling bathroom drain) cost $100 to $300 to clear with a hand-fed or drum cable machine. Main sewer line snaking, run through an exterior cleanout out to the city connection, usually runs $150 to $350. Hydro jetting or root cutting on a main line typically falls between $350 and $900, depending on how much grease or root mass has to be cut out and whether a follow-up camera inspection is included.

Those ranges reflect what published national cost guides report for residential drain work, not a quote for your specific line. A technician can't price root cutting or jetting accurately without seeing the pipe first, which is why a real quote usually starts with a [sewer camera inspection](/services/sewer-camera-inspection/) rather than a phone-estimate guess.

Single-fixture work is the cheapest because the access point is right there (the drain itself, or a nearby P-trap) and the cable run is short. Main-line work costs more because the technician needs a cleanout, has to run cable or jetting hose much farther, and may need a second trip if roots or a collapsed section show up on camera.

## Why Do Two Drain Cleaning Quotes for the Same Clog Come in So Far Apart?

Two companies can quote wildly different numbers for what sounds like the same clog because they're not actually pricing the same job. The biggest swing factors are cleanout access, how far the blockage sits from that access point, and whether the call falls inside normal business hours or after.

A home with an accessible two-way cleanout in the yard or driveway is a straightforward snake job. A home without one, common in older South Corona tract houses built before cleanouts were standard code, may require pulling a toilet or accessing the line through a roof vent, which adds labor time and usually a higher price. Time of day matters too. ## When Do You Need Hydro Jetting or Root Cutting Instead of a Standard Snake?

A cable snake punches a hole through a clog; hydro jetting scours the entire inside diameter of the pipe with high-pressure water, which is why it costs more and lasts longer on grease buildup and root intrusion. Root cutting is usually bundled into a jetting job when a camera inspection shows roots have grown into a joint or crack.

This matters more in Corona than it might elsewhere in the Inland Empire. A lot of housing stock near Historic Downtown and South Corona still runs on original vitrified clay sewer laterals from the 1960s and 70s, and clay joints are exactly what tree roots look for. Newer construction out toward Eagle Glen and Dos Lagos is more likely on PVC, which is far more root-resistant but not immune to grease buildup from years of kitchen use.

If you've had the same drain clog twice in six months, a snake probably isn't solving the root cause (sometimes literally). That pattern is the clearest sign jetting and a camera inspection are worth the extra cost over another round of snaking. ## What Should a Written Drain Cleaning Quote Include Before the Truck Leaves?

A real quote names the method (cable snake, jetting, or both), the access point being used, and a firm price for the main line specifically, not just the service-call fee. Ask for the main-line number before anyone starts, not after the truck is already parked and the cleanout is open.

A quote worth trusting should also spell out whether a camera inspection is included or billed separately, what happens if roots or a pipe defect show up mid-job, and whether there's any warranty on the clear (30 to 90 days is common for jetting work, since it removes buildup rather than just punching through it). If chemical drain cleaners got you into this mess to begin with, our piece on [chemical cleaners vs. calling a plumber](/blog/drain-cleaning-diy-vs-pro/) explains why they often make the eventual professional job more expensive, not less.

If a quote won't give you a firm main-line number up front, or wants to start work before showing you a camera scan on a recurring clog, treat that as a red flag rather than a bargain.
