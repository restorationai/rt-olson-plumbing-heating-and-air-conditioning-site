**TL;DR:** Emergency plumber cost depends more on timing and repair type than on the word "emergency." Expect a trip or dispatch fee that's typically higher after normal business hours, on weekends, and on holidays, on top of the cost of the actual repair. A 24/7 plumber should quote both pieces, including any after-hours premium, before anyone touches a pipe. Get that number in writing before work starts, even at 2 a.m.

## How much does an emergency plumber cost?

Emergency plumber cost is built from two separate pieces: a dispatch or trip fee for coming out outside normal hours, and the price of the actual repair, which gets quoted once the plumber has eyes on the problem. The dispatch fee covers getting a licensed technician and a stocked van to your door at 11 p.m. instead of 11 a.m. The repair cost depends entirely on what's actually wrong.

A running toilet or a stuck garbage disposal costs far less to fix than a failed water heater or a slab leak under a tile floor, after-hours rate or not. Harder-to-access repairs (a water line inside a wall, a cleanout buried under landscaping) also take longer, which shows up in the labor line. For a baseline sense of what individual repairs cost during normal business hours, our [general plumber cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-plumber-cost/) breaks down typical jobs; an after-hours call adds the dispatch premium on top of those same repair costs.

## Do plumbers charge more on weekends and holidays?

Yes. Most plumbing companies, RT Olson included, charge the same after-hours rate for weekend and holiday calls that they charge for a weekday night call, since the crew is working outside their scheduled hours either way. A Saturday afternoon call isn't automatically cheaper than a Tuesday 2 a.m. call just because the sun is up.

The after-hours premium exists because a technician is being pulled off personal time to respond, not because your specific leak is worth more at night. ## What should an emergency plumber's quote include?

A proper emergency quote itemizes the dispatch fee separately from the repair, states the diagnosis in plain language, and lists parts and labor before work begins. If a plumber can't tell you the dispatch fee and a repair range over the phone or at your door, before cutting into anything, that's a red flag, not a quirk of emergency work.

Before you let anyone start, the quote should cover:

- The after-hours dispatch or trip fee, stated as its own line
- What was actually diagnosed, not just "leak" or "clog"
- Parts and labor broken out, so you can see what's driving the total
- Any warranty on the repair and the parts
- The company's license number, so you can verify it yourself

California's Contractors State License Board publishes a [consumer guide to hiring a licensed contractor](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/) that walks through exactly what to check before you authorize work, which is worth a minute even during a midnight emergency. If you want the full rundown on verifying a plumbing license yourself, see [how to verify a licensed California plumber](/blog/licensed-plumber-c36-verify-california/).

## How can you keep the cost down on an after-hours call?

The fastest way to lower an emergency bill is to stop the water before the plumber arrives and describe the problem clearly on the phone, since both reduce the time a technician spends diagnosing instead of fixing. A clear description also lets dispatch send a tech with the right parts already on the van, which avoids a second trip.

A few things that actually move the number:

- Shut off the water at the fixture or the main, so active damage stops accumulating while you wait
- Tell the dispatcher exactly what's happening (steady drip vs. spraying, one fixture vs. whole house) so the right tech and parts get sent
- Mention any other plumbing issue you've been putting off; bundling a second small repair into the same after-hours trip usually costs less than a separate visit later
- Ask directly whether the issue can safely hold until morning. A reputable 24/7 plumber will tell you honestly if a repair can wait, even though that means less revenue tonight

## When is it worth paying the after-hours rate instead of waiting?

It's worth paying the after-hours rate when water, sewage, or gas is actively causing damage or creating a health or safety risk, and not worth it for a slow drain or a minor drip that can hold until daytime rates apply. A steady supply line leak soaking a cabinet floor is a tonight problem. A toilet that runs for thirty extra seconds after flushing is not.

Call right away for: active flooding, a sewage backup into a tub or floor drain, no water to the whole house, or a water heater leaking at the tank itself. These situations get worse by the hour and the after-hours premium is cheaper than the water damage. If you ever smell gas near a water heater, furnace, or gas line, leave the house immediately, don't flip any switches, and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency line from outside before calling anyone else, including a plumber. A licensed plumber's job starts only after the utility confirms the scene is safe. If water has already spread into drywall or flooring, a licensed plumber stops the source; drying out the structure and checking for mold is work for your insurer or a restoration professional, separate from the plumbing repair itself.
