## Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

A leaking water heater is an emergency only if the leak is fast and continuous, you smell gas near the unit, or the water is undermining flooring, drywall, or an attached room. A slow drip from a fitting or the drain valve can usually wait for a same-day appointment, not a 2 a.m. dispatch.

Corona's water heaters work harder than average because of the Inland Empire's hard water. Mineral buildup inside the tank corrodes the glass lining faster and strains the lower heating element or burner, so leaks often show up around year 8 to 10 rather than waiting for a full 12 to 15 year lifespan. That doesn't make every leak urgent, it just means replacement is often already on the table once one shows up, something we break down in [how long water heaters last and when to repair vs replace](/blog/water-heater-lifespan-repair-or-replace/).

If you smell gas anywhere near the unit, treat it as a true emergency regardless of how the leak looks. Leave the house immediately, don't flip light switches or anything else that could spark, and call 911 or SoCalGas's emergency line from outside. Only after the utility confirms the gas is safe should you call a licensed plumber to repair the line or pressure test it. A plumber handles the repair after the gas company clears the scene, never instead of it.

## How do you shut off the water, gas, or power to a leaking water heater right now?

Turn the cold water supply valve on top of the tank clockwise until it stops, then turn the gas control dial to "pilot" on a gas unit or switch off the dedicated breaker for an electric unit. That stops the leak from getting worse and keeps the heating element or burner from firing on a tank that's losing water.

1. Find the cold water inlet valve, an oval handle or ball valve on the pipe entering the top of the tank, and turn it clockwise until fully closed.
2. On gas units, turn the gas control knob on the front of the tank to "pilot," not fully off. That keeps the pilot lit without the main burner firing on a nearly empty tank.
3. On electric units, go to the breaker panel and flip the dedicated water heater breaker, usually a double-pole 30-amp breaker, to off.
4. If water is pooling near outlets or the panel, skip the breaker and call a plumber instead. Don't wade into standing water near electrical equipment.
5. Open a hot water tap somewhere in the house to relieve pressure in the lines, then grab towels or a shop vac for what's already on the floor.

## Where is the leak actually coming from?

Water heater leaks come from four places: the top fittings (supply lines or the cold water inlet), the temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve, the drain valve near the bottom, or the tank itself through a corroded seam, and only the last one means the tank is done.

Top fittings are the easiest fix. A loose compression nut or a worn dielectric union usually just needs re-sealing or replacing, often under $200 in parts and labor. A dripping T&P valve is either a failed valve itself (common, and a straightforward swap) or a sign tank pressure is genuinely too high from a failed expansion tank or a closed-loop plumbing system, worth a proper diagnosis rather than just capping the valve. A leaking drain valve, the small spigot near the bottom used to flush the tank, is almost always a bad valve or loose cap, also a simple repair.

Water coming from underneath the tank itself, or rust-colored water pooling on the floor beneath the steel shell, means the inner tank has corroded through. There's no patch for that. The tank gets replaced, not repaired, every time.

## When does a leaking tank mean replacement instead of repair?

Replace, don't repair, when the leak comes from the tank body itself, when the unit is past 8 to 10 years old in Corona's hard water, or when a repair estimate runs close to half the cost of a new unit. A fitting or valve leak on a younger tank is usually worth repairing.

Check the serial number on the manufacturer's rating plate. The first four digits are usually the manufacture date. A tank leaking at 9 or 10 years old with visible scale on the anode rod almost always makes more financial sense to replace than repair, since the next failure is close behind.
