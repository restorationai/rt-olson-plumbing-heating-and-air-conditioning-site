If your dishwasher just let go and water is spreading across the kitchen floor right now, find the angle stop under the sink, the small oval valve on the cold line feeding the unit, and turn it clockwise until it stops. That single step buys you time to figure out whether you're looking at a $20 fill valve, a $40 supply line, or something that needs a plumber to open the wall or cabinet. ## What causes a dishwasher to leak and flood a kitchen?

Most dishwasher floods trace to one of four points: a failed supply line or fill valve, a cracked or worn door gasket, a clogged or kinked drain hose that backs water into the tub, or a float switch that never tells the unit to stop filling. Any one of these can dump gallons onto the floor in minutes, especially overnight when nobody is around to catch it early.

The supply line is the most common culprit in homes over 10 years old, particularly braided steel lines that corrode at the fitting. A worn door gasket leaks slowly during the wash cycle and often gets blamed on "the floor just being wet," until it adds up to warped cabinet toe-kicks and soft subflooring. A clogged drain hose is a close second: food debris or a kinked hose under the sink backs wash water up and over the tub lip instead of draining out.

## What should you do in the first 10 minutes after you spot the leak?

Shut off the water at the angle stop under the sink, then unplug the dishwasher at the outlet or breaker if any water has reached an electrical connection. Working fast and in the right order limits both the flood and any electrical risk.

1. Turn the angle stop under the sink clockwise (this is the dishwasher's dedicated shutoff, separate from the sink faucet's valves).
2. Cut power to the unit at the breaker if the outlet or cord sits in standing water. Never reach into wet areas near a live outlet.
3. Mop and towel up standing water immediately, including inside the cabinet toe-kick and under the dishwasher itself.
4. Pull the toe-kick panel and check for water pooled under the unit; a wet-dry vac speeds this up.
5. Photograph the damage and the appliance's data plate (model and serial number) before you move anything, for your insurance file.

## Is dishwasher flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

A sudden, accidental failure of a dishwasher supply line or hose is usually covered by a standard homeowners policy, the same way a burst pipe is. Gradual leaks from a worn gasket that went unnoticed for months are a different story and are often treated as maintenance, not a covered loss. Coverage language varies by insurer, so review your own policy or ask your agent before assuming either way (see [what is typically covered by standard homeowners insurance](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance)).

One detail worth knowing: water that has already passed through the dishwasher's wash cycle carries detergent and food residue, which puts it closer to gray water than clean supply water, even though the leak may have started as a clean-water failure. If water sat under cabinets for more than a day or two, have it checked rather than assuming it dried out fine on its own, since [mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## When do you need a plumber instead of an appliance repair technician?

Call a plumber when the leak is coming from the supply line, the shutoff valve, or anywhere in the home's fixed plumbing rather than the appliance itself. An appliance repair technician handles the dishwasher's internal parts (the pump, the door gasket, the float switch); a plumber handles the angle stop, the supply line, the drain connection at the garbage disposal or air gap, and any water that has made it into the wall or subfloor. If you can't tell which side of that line the problem is on, that's worth a [leak detection](/services/leak-detection/) visit rather than guessing.

If the water pressure at that angle stop is low, or the shutoff won't fully close, that valve likely needs replacing before the dishwasher goes back online. Our guide on [how to find a water leak](/blog/how-to-find-a-water-leak/) walks through the broader process if the source isn't obvious at first.

A kitchen flood that keeps a cabinet or floor wet for more than a day is also worth a second look even after the water stops, since trapped moisture under cabinetry doesn't always dry on its own. [Request an emergency plumbing visit](/services/emergency-plumbing/) if the shutoff valve itself is the failure point, or if water has reached a wall or adjoining room.
