## Restoration emergencies common in Silverado

Silverado Canyon sits in the foothills of the Santa Ana Mountains, and that geography drives most of the plumbing and mechanical problems we see. Homes built along the canyon slopes often deal with shifting soil after seasonal rain, which can stress buried supply lines and drain runs that weren't engineered for movement. Many properties here rely on well water, propane, or septic systems rather than the full municipal infrastructure found in nearby Orange County cities, so water heaters and softeners work against higher mineral content, and drain lines back up differently than a standard sewer-connected home. Wildfire risk in the canyon also means HVAC systems need reliable filtration and shutoffs, since ash and smoke exposure during fire season is a real seasonal driver here, not an abstract concern.

Silverado Canyon's homes range from older rustic cabins to newer hillside construction, and that age spread matters: older cabins may still carry galvanized supply lines or outdated drain materials that are well past their service life, while newer builds are more likely to run copper or PEX.
