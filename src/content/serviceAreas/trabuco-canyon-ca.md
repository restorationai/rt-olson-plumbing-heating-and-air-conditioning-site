## Restoration emergencies common in Trabuco Canyon

Trabuco Canyon sits in the foothills of the Santa Ana Mountains, and that geography shapes what goes wrong in a home here. Steep, winding lots mean more buried supply and drain runs under driveways and retaining walls, which makes a slow leak harder to spot until a hillside starts staying damp or a retaining wall shows efflorescence. Dry Santa Ana wind conditions and wildfire-season air quality put extra load on HVAC filtration, and homes on well or septic systems need plumbing work that accounts for private water quality rather than a municipal main. Winter brings infrequent but real freeze risk on exposed hose bibs and above-ground piping on north-facing slopes, which is enough to crack a line that was never insulated for it.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Trabuco Canyon

Much of Trabuco Canyon's housing was built from the 1970s through the 1990s, with custom and semi-custom homes on larger hillside parcels, typically slab-on-grade or stepped foundations that follow the slope. Plumbing in that era is usually copper supply with ABS or cast iron drain lines, and older sections may still carry galvanized runs that corrode from the inside out long before a visible leak appears. The area's soil is a mix of decomposed granite and expansive clay depending on elevation, which shifts seasonally with rain and can stress buried lines and foundations differently than flatland tracts nearby.
