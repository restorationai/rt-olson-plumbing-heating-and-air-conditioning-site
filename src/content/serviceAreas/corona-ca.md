## Restoration emergencies common in Corona

Corona sits in a part of Riverside County that swings hard between seasons, triple-digit summer heat that runs air conditioners nonstop and strains aging condensing units, followed by winter storm systems that can overwhelm a yard's drainage and push groundwater against foundations built decades ago. Homes on older galvanized or original copper supply lines are more prone to pinhole leaks and slab leaks as the pipe material fatigues, and those failures often go unnoticed until a water bill spikes or a warm spot shows up underfoot on a slab floor. Water heaters installed in the late 1990s and early 2000s building boom are also reaching or past typical service life across the city, and a tank that starts rumbling with sediment is usually a matter of when, not if, it fails.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Corona

Much of Corona's older housing stock near the historic core dates to the mid-20th century, built slab-on-grade with galvanized or early copper supply lines, materials that are now well past their expected service life and prone to pinhole leaks and corrosion at joints. Newer subdivisions built from the 1980s onward generally use copper or PEX supply lines and are less prone to that specific failure mode, though slab foundations remain the norm across most of the city regardless of build era. Riverside County's soils include areas of expansive clay that shift with seasonal moisture, which can stress slab foundations and underground plumbing runs over time.
